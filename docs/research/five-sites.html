Five sites, one standard

What makes the five portfolios you picked feel expensive, measured screen by screen, and what that means for a site made with Higgsfield and Claude that shows your journey, your robotics and your taste.

lucerra.co vaulk.com huyml.co christoph-nagel.dev cartooneast.in

The short answer

They sell one idea, in produced imagery, with almost no interface

  • One concept, end to endA monolith. A shelter seen in x-ray. A red serif name and a flying card stack. A black-and-white face and one huge word. A felt puppet of the designer. Each site is one idea, repeated with discipline.
  • Imagery is produced, not drawnPhotoreal renders, graded photography, cinematic video, a consistent character. None of them draw their main picture in code. That's the biggest gap in what I built.
  • The interface nearly disappearsTiny corner labels, a menu, an email address. The picture and one sentence do the work.
  • Few sections, short copyFour to six sections, not sixteen. "Code. Kamera. Klartext." "Brands grow. Simple." "Digital experiences, directed as cinema."
  • Motion is cinematic and scarceA loader, a camera move, a video in a frame, one signature interaction. Mostly video, with a little WebGL where it counts.

Teardowns

What each one does, and what to take

Lucerrá

Creative studio · Nuxt, video · WebGL landscape

One iconic object, lit like a film, and nothing else on screen.

Why it feels expensive

A loader that counts to 100% over a single white sliver, then a camera move into the scene. Sound and "spatial context" toggles. The name is set small. It trusts the image.

Take

A single hero object with real lighting. The loader as the opening shot. Work shown as film in a frame. Restraint on type size.

Don't copy

The abstract mystery. Recruiters must know within five seconds that you build robots.

Vaulk

Protective shelters · Nuxt, Lenis, OGL, Rive · Telegraf + Chivo Mono

Beauty shot first, engineering x-ray second.

Why it feels expensive

The render is indistinguishable from a photo. The technical section is dark, gridded and precise, with small mono labels and a side index of chapters. Credibility through engineering detail.

Take

Your car as the product: photoreal first, then an x-ray of its real stack (LiDAR, YOLO, Jetson, Nav2), with callouts. This fits a robotics portfolio better than anything else here.

Note

It uses Chivo Mono, orange callouts and wireframes, the same ingredients as my drafting sheet. The difference is execution: photoreal imagery, a dark ground, and restraint.

Huy Phan · huyml

Designer, Ho Chi Minh City · Framer, OGL, Rive, GSAP · BT Glyphius + BT Grotesk

Personality in the intro, rigour in the index.

Why it feels expensive

A characterful serif at poster size, then a calm grey canvas where the work flies past as tilted cards. Every project carries role, date and recognition. The avatar returns as a tiny logo.

Take

A signature work index you'd remember. Proof on every project: role, year, result. A personal mark that recurs.

Don't copy

The illustrated character style: it's his. Yours should come from your world (robots, telescopes, circuits).

Christoph Nagel

Developer and photographer · WordPress, GSAP · Anton

A great photograph of you beats any effect.

Why it feels expensive

Simple tech, strong photography: one grade (black and white), consistent light, the person in every frame. The type is huge and condensed, the words are short.

Take

A real portrait session in one consistent grade, and a verb for each part of your life. It's the cheapest way to look premium.

Don't copy

The slideshow structure. Your story is a journey across years, so it wants a timeline.

Cartooneast

Anurag Bandyopadhyay, visual designer · Next.js, video loops, Rive

A consistent character of you carries the whole story.

Why it works

The character appears to be AI-generated and animated as short videos. It stays perfectly consistent: same face, jacket and felt texture in every pose. That consistency is what makes it look designed, not generated.

Take

This is the closest to "made with Higgsfield and Claude": one identity, many poses, each pose telling a chapter.

Watch

Your own rule currently bans AI images of your face in the hero and "now" scenes. A stylised likeness still counts. It's your call to change that.

The standard

Six things all five do, and where my build missed

Produced hero imagery

Render, photograph, film or character: made with a camera's standards.

I drew diagrams and dots in code. They read as placeholders.

One idea, repeated

Every screen belongs to the same world.

Sixteen scenes, sixteen different visuals.

Almost no chrome

Corner labels, a menu, an email. Nothing else competes.

FIG. numbers, source tags, rules and captions everywhere.

Four to six sections

Short, confident, easy to finish.

Sixteen chapters: too long to feel special.

Copy with a voice

Three words can be a whole section.

Descriptive captions that explain instead of land.

Motion like a camera

A loader, a push-in, a cut. Mostly video.

Constant morphing points: busy, not cinematic.

Three concepts for you

Each one idea, each one Higgsfield pipeline

Recommended

The Machine

Lucerrá's single lit object meets Vaulk's beauty-then-x-ray. Robotics first, cinematic, realistic.

  1. OpeningBlack screen. The loader counts LiDAR points instead of percent. Your autonomous car appears, rim-lit in a dark studio, slowly turning. One line and your name, set small.
  2. X-rayOn scroll the car's shell turns to wireframe and its real perception comes alive: YOLO boxes, LiDAR sweep, callouts for Jetson Orin Nano, Nav2, Cartographer. Credibility, Vaulk-style.
  3. JourneyFour wide film frames: Dhaka, Ohio, Hilton and Airbnb, now. Places and objects only, people from behind. One sentence each.
  4. WorkA signature index: project previews as moving frames with role, year and result.
  5. AboutA real portrait in one grade (the Nagel lesson), a short paragraph, contact.

Higgsfield does

A 3D model of your real car from 1–4 photos (image-to-3D, GLB), studio beauty clips (Cinema Studio or Kling), the journey frames (Nano Banana Pro stills plus short motion).

Claude does

The site: real-time car on the page (three.js), the scroll camera, the x-ray layer, index, copy, performance.

You provide

4 clean photos of the car (front, side, back, three-quarter). One real portrait session; a friend's phone at night by a window is enough.

Face rule

Works as is: your face is only in the real portrait.

The Film

Nagel's single huge word per chapter meets Lucerrá's cinema. Your journey as a five-act short film.

  1. Acts"Dhaka." "Ohio." "Build." "Ship." "Now." Each is one full-bleed cinematic shot in a single grade, with a giant word and one line.
  2. HeroA real video portrait of you, now.
  3. PastPhotoreal scenes of you in the past, generated with a trained Soul ID (20+ photos of you): leaving Dhaka, arriving in Ohio, founding the club.
  4. WorkEach project opens like a scene.

Higgsfield does

Soul ID of you, cinematic stills, 6–8 motion shots (Cinema Studio 3.0 is 25 credits for 5 seconds).

You provide

20+ varied photos of your face for the Soul ID, and a short real video of you for the hero.

Face rule

Allowed only shot by shot for past scenes, with your approval each time. It's the most expensive of the three.

The Character

Cartooneast's consistent self-character meets Huyml's editorial rigour. Memorable and warm.

  1. IdeaA collectible-figure version of you (matte vinyl or clay, your glasses and jacket), doing your life: soldering a Jetson, launching the rover, at the OWU telescope, holding your diploma.
  2. MotionShort loops of the figure per section, on a calm ground with a characterful serif.
  3. WorkHuyml-style card stack with proof on every project.

Higgsfield does

A reference element or Soul of you, then stylised stills (cheap) and short loops (Kling 3.0 pro, 8.75 credits for 5 seconds).

You provide

A handful of clear photos of your face and clothes.

Face rule

Needs a change: a stylised likeness is still AI-generated you, in the hero.

Recommendation

Build The Machine

It's the one only you can have. Anyone can train an AI likeness or grade a portrait. Nobody else has your car, your perception stack and your LiDAR. Leading with a real machine you built, shot like a product film, says "robotics engineer" in five seconds and looks as expensive as Vaulk or Lucerrá.

Borrow from the others: Nagel's single real portrait for About, Huyml's proof on every project, and Cartooneast's warmth in the copy.

What I need from you

Four answers, then a mood board, not a website

  1. Which concept: The Machine, The Film, The Character, or a mix you describe.
  2. Which screen from the five sites made you feel "yes, that"? The frames above are there to point at.
  3. Your car: can you take 4 clean photos of it (front, side, back, three-quarter) on a plain floor?
  4. Your face rule: keep it as it is, or loosen it (only needed for The Film or The Character).

Next step after that is cheap and visual: a mood board of 6–8 Higgsfield stills in the chosen world, for you to approve before any code.
