The current tokens. Warm cream, near-black, one hot accent: the exact cluster generated sites fall into.

  • Cream paper and one orangeReads as "tasteful template", not as a person. Nothing about it says robotics, space or 3D.
  • Drafting-sheet devices everywhere"FIG. 04", [REAL]/[CODE] tags, hairline rules and condensed labels on all sixteen scenes. Clever once; wallpaper by the third.
  • One layout, sixteen timesText left, picture right, every chapter. The story has no pacing: no quiet beat, no full-bleed moment, no climax.
  • Stipple, not splatsThe clouds are flat dots on paper: no light, no depth of field, no glow. Real Gaussian splats look photographic and luminous; these look like clip art.

The technology

Real Gaussian splats, not points drawn from a photo

What's on the site now is a 2.5D trick: a photo, a depth guess and dots. A real splat is a 3D capture you can walk around, made from a short phone video.

Now

Depth-map point clouds

  • One photo per portrait, flat from the side
  • Uniform dots, no lighting or translucency
  • 36K points; cheap, but reads as stipple
  • Good as a loading state: coarse points first

Proposed

Captured 3DGS, rendered with Spark 2.0

  • Open-source three.js renderer from World Labs
  • Streams huge splats with level of detail; loads a coarse version first, then refines
  • Shader effects on splats: dissolve, reveal, relight, clip
  • Web budget: about 15–30 MB on phones, compressed (SPZ, .RAD)

What you would capture (phone + Scaniverse or Polycam, free, about 2 minutes each)

  • You, seated, stillSlow orbit, even light. The hero: a real 3D you, not an AI one.
  • The autonomous carOn a table, full orbit. Pairs with the LiDAR chapter.

Recommendation

Night Capture, with two Radiance chapters

Build on A · Night Capture: dark, luminous and restrained, so real splats glow the way they are meant to, and the type does the talking. Borrow two full-bleed moments from C: the flight, and the LiDAR fly-through into the work. Use the LiDAR intensity ramp as the only data colour. That's the one detail no other portfolio can have.

Keep the current point clouds as the loading state: coarse points that resolve into the full splat. That's the storyboard's own "resolves out of points" idea, made literal.

Keep

  • The story, its order, every fact
  • Scroll never taken over; reduced motion respected
  • Real captures only for your face; no AI likeness
  • Performance budget: fast first paint, 3D after

Drop

  • Cream paper, the orange, the drafting-sheet frame
  • "FIG." eyebrows and [REAL]/[CODE] tags in the page (sourcing moves to the colophon)
  • The same text-left, picture-right layout sixteen times
  • Flat stipple as the final look

What I need from you

Three answers, then one prototype

  1. Which direction: A, B, C, or the recommended mix.
  2. Will you capture splats? Your portrait first; the car and bench if you can. Two minutes each on your phone.
  3. Two or three sites you love, from anywhere. They'll calibrate taste faster than anything I can write.

Then I build only the hero, in the chosen direction, for you to judge before anything else changes.
